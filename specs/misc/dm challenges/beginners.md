Beginner DM's first daredevil dive
==================================

A new and enthusiastic DM wants to convince two of his friends to play DnD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Brett the aspiring DM, Judy, and Judy's boyfriend Tony are sitting around a table. They all have a couple of hours to fill, so Brett comes up with the rather daring idea to try and get them interested in a "short" game of DnD.

### Kick-off conversation

- Judy (soon-to-be Player 1): So, how does one play Dungeons and Dragons? What do we need?
- Brett (DM-in-training): Well, not much... a set of dice, a story book, a couple of rulebooks, a table, pen and paper...
- Tony (soon-to-be Player 2): Ow snap. I thought we could give it a go right now... 
- Brett: Oh but we can! In fact, we could begin with just pen and paper, but I happen to have my tablet with me, so I can use the DM app.
- Judy: So your app replaces everything you just listed?
- Brett: Pretty much, but we don't even need it all to begin. So Judy - who are you gonna be?
- Judy: Pardon me?
- Brett: Well, it's a roleplaying game, so you will be playing a hero character. You both of course - you too, Tony! So Judy, what kind of hero would you like to be? A fighter? A healer? A witch - well, a female wizard? There's loads of options, but I'd advise you to choose something basic. Don't forget I'm a beginner as a DM.
- Judy: A DM? What's that?
- Brett: Well, that's how DnD works. We all play the game, but one person has to be the so-called Dungeon Master. The players each play their hero character, but the DM plays everybody else.
- Judy: The DM plays everybody else? But that's ridiculous - he would be guaranteed to win!
- Brett: Ah but you see, DMs cannot "win". They have to run the game, so they're not competing. But in fact, the players aren't really competing either. We're all, the DM included, entering a world in our imaginations; and the goal is not really to "win", but to experience an adventure together.
- Judy: Sounds really strange, but interesting too. Ok, I'm game. I'd like to be a - hm, I'd like to be a detective! Is that possible?
- Brett (slightly taken aback): Uh, hm, well. Well actually, a "detective" is not really part of the classes... well never mind that, actually. Why not? It means your character should be smart and perceptive, right? No problem. But how would he or she fight?
- Judy: Fight? Why should she have to fight? 
- Brett (getting a little nervous now because he's just realized that he'll be expected to present Judy with a mystery to solve): Well, in DnD, heroes nearly always have to fight sooner or later. Or at least to overcome dangers of various kinds.
- Judy: Oh, ok. Hm, I guess a detective should have a revolver, so I'll take that.
- Brett (taking a deep breath to prepare for potentially long explanations): Well you see, DnD takes place in a kind of medieval world. Pistols do exist, but they're not as effective as in our world. For self-defense, unless you're a wizard, you have to rely on some kind of weapon. With the exception of the fighting "monks" who do unarmed combat - you know, kinda Kung-Fu style.
- Tony (lifting an eyebrow and making a sound for the first time): Hm...!
- Judy: Do I really *have* to fight? Can't I just be, like, the one in the group who solves all the mysteries, while the others do the fighting?
- Tony: ... which would be me, since there's only the three of us, and Brett won't do any fighting - right?
- Brett: Ah, yes, true. But it could work! But Judy, even if you're gonna be the intellectual, you'll have to be able to hold your own in a fight. Even if Tony chooses a die-hard fighter class, he's still going to start at level 1, and that means he would have his hands full against just 2 or three ruffians, and would not be able to protect you. So, let me think... hm, tell me: would your "detective" be the type that is good at reading and interrogating people, or were you thinking more like Sherlock Holmes, you know, with the magnifying glass, analyzing footprints, cigarette ash, etc.?
- Judy: Well, that's an easy one - I'm definitely the people skills person!
- Tony: ... yeah, right! ...
- Brett (quickly): ... guys, no need to fight over this. It's a game, and we can make it work. Judy, this means you should actually play a *cleric*. No, hear me out: there are all kinds of clerics in DnD, because there are all kinds of deities. Minor ones, understand? We're not talking about *the* God here. Now I can't remember which one of those deities would best fit a detective type, but trust me on this one, I'll just look it up. Now: people skills, at least in DnD, are dependent on wisdom, which is also the attribute that powers the kind of magic that clerics use. So, you could be a cleric... - let's say, a novice sent out by the abbott to solve a mystery...
- Judy: Oooh! A murder in the abbey!
- Brett (feeling that things are getting out of hand real quick): ...uh, perhaps. Let's not make things too difficult for ..., uh I mean let's not start out too complicated, ok? Now Tony, what would you like to play? ("Please, please, don't let him have any fancy ideas!")
- Tony: Oh, I don't know... there's going to be fights, right? Well, I want to be a guy who's good at fighting.
- Brett ("Oh thank you Lord", with a wide smile of relief): Oh yes, Tony, there will be fights (I'll make sure of that). So, a good solid fighter for you? You don't mind using real weapons like swords and the like, do you?
- Tony: Well, the kung-fu idea sounded kinda interesting, but hey, I like the swords and shields stuff. Just let me be the guy people don't wanna mess with!
- Brett ("Phew!"): Oh we can absolutely do that! Ok, now just a few details to get out of the way - your names, general appearance and so on, and then we'll be good to go.

This conversation is actually the transition between the pitch and "sesion zero", whether the players know the term or not. What needs to follow now is Brett whipping out pencil and notebook OR the DM app (OR both), and the three of them creating the player characters together. Other than their names, the group needs to define the PCs races/species, main ability scores and skills, their starting equipment, plus a few lines about their backgrounds. Much can be left open for later though.

This is where the DM app absolutely *has* to shine. It must provide aspiring DMs with all the information they need, and the means to input them fast and effortlessly - with gentle guidance but hardly any hard constraints (since pencil & notebook would not impose any either).

**:bulb: Ideas at this point:**
- Ability scores represented by rectangles of varying height to reflect their percentage of the total budget
- Color-coded
- Primary and secondary ability scores marked with one or two asterisks or equivalent highlighting
- Sub-par ability scores for a chosen class can be annotated by the DM
- Selecting the Cleric class will bring up a choice of deities, each with sufficient information for the player or DM to decide whether it matches the PC
