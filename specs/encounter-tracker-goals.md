The Encounter Tracker view
==========================

Goals and expectations
----------------------

### Introduction: the need for an Encounter Tracker

To be perfectly honest, there is no absolute *need* for a software encounter tracker: tracking DnD combat encounters will always ultimately be the responsibility of the DM (Dungeon Master). The whole ambition of this project however is to provide a tool that will enable the DM to conduct combat encounters *faster* and with less headache than purely on paper - which is a HUGE challenge, because any software (with the possible exception of a full AI, which would introduce its own set of challenges) will inevitably introduce constraints that can only be made acceptable by also offering conveniences and possibilities that neither a cleverly formatted sheet of paper nor, say, a well-designed spreadsheet can. 

That latter objective may be near-impossible to achieve: spreadsheets are incredibly powerful and fast, and a tablet-targetting app such as DM Screen only has very few advantages to try and compensate for that: more flexibility in visual presentation and the ability to work with nested data structures, and the *possibility* - not out of the box, mind you - to work around the absence of a keyboard, potentially freeing the DM from needing both hands on the keyboard to conduct a game. That is, in short, the whole bet behind this app: giving DMs a tool that they will prefer over manipulating a spreadsheet.

Ideally, the app should also replace online lookup resources such D&D Compendium, Roll20, etc.. But THAT now is an unrealistic hope: the very best that can be achieved may be that DM Screen does not *get in the way* of using such compendiums... too much.

Why so much talk about a single view of the planned DM Screen app, by the way? Well, because the Encounter Tracker is the centerpiece and motivation behind the whole app, owing to the fact that combat is often time-consuming and can feel like it slows a game down. This app absolutely *must* make it much easier for a DM to conduct encounters, so that neither the DM nor the players need dread it: combat must be fun, ideally as much or more so than in a PC game adaptation of DnD.

### How to make combat easy for the DM and players

Combat in DnD is the most precisely regulated aspect of DnD, and as such will inevitably feel daunting for beginner DMs, and likely remain stressful even as DMs progress - because the games they run will almost certainly become more demanding as well (if not in complexity, then certainly in the standards DMs will expect of themselves).

So, how to help DMs? By guiding them without restraining them; by putting all relevant information at their fingertips; and doing it all in ways that are both lightning-fast and safe at the same time; and by always showing them everything they need to know in every givent moment and situation without requiring them to navigate back and forth.

Those principles may be impossible to achieve at 100%. But this app must try.

### The most important workflow: the straight dive without preparation

This may sound extreme, but it isn't: DnD takes place in shared imagined worlds, and it absolutely *must* be possible to start a game without any preparation whatsoever. Anything less would be a fun killer.
